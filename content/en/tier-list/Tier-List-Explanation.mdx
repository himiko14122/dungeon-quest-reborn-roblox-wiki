## Common Mistakes When Using Tier Lists

The first and most prevalent mistake involves treating tier rankings as absolute determinations rather than contextual recommendations. An item's placement reflects specific evaluation criteria that may not align with your current goals, skill level, or preferred playstyle. Blindly pursuing S tier weapons while ignoring the gameplay context that makes those rankings meaningful often leads to frustration and inefficient farming decisions.

Another common error concerns **over-farming for marginal upgrades** based on tier list preferences that exceed practical significance. The difference between an A tier and S tier weapon might amount to only 5-10% damage increase in most scenarios, which rarely justifies extensive farming when A tier options already enable comfortable progression. Players who obsess over perfect tier placements often spend far more time farming than necessary, missing opportunities to enjoy the actual gameplay content.

Ignoring class-specific considerations creates another category of tier list misuse. A weapon that dominates Warrior builds might rank significantly lower for Mage characters due to different scaling mechanics and ability synergies. Applying generic tier rankings without considering your specific class often results in suboptimal equipment choices that fail to leverage your character's unique strengths and mechanics.

**Accessibility neglect** represents a subtle but significant mistake when using tier lists for planning purposes. S tier weapons that drop only from the hardest bosses in the game may theoretically be the best options, but if those bosses prove impenetrable with your current gear and skill level, they offer zero practical value. Balance aspirational tier targets against realistic acquisition chances when planning your farming routes.
